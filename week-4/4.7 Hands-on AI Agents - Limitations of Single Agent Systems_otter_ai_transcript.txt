As we conclude this lab, it's important to reflect on the limitations of what we have built, and also understand how we might address those limitations in more advanced implementations. The single agent system that we constructed performed reasonably well when all of the required data can fit into a single static prompt. However, this approach does have several significant drawbacks. First, it struggles with tasks involving dynamic, frequently changing, or large data sets because we have to reload all of the data every time we make a query. Second, it cannot reliably execute deterministic logic or structured operations within the prompt alone. The model is interpreting our business rules from natural language, which can sometimes lead to inconsistencies. Third, and perhaps the most critically important point, is that language models have strict context window limits. There is only so much text that you can include in a prompt, and as your customer database grows to 1000s or millions of records, it becomes impossible to load full tables, long histories, or evolving records at scale. And then finally, when the model is forced to infer data instead of fetching it from a reliable source, there is a risk of inaccuracies or hallucinations. The solution to these limitations is to give our agents access to tools. Tools are external capabilities that the agent can invoke to perform specific operations. Instead of stuffing all of the data into the prompt, tools provide controlled access to databases, APIs, and computational resources, allowing the agent to pull only the precise data that it needs and when it needs it. Tools enable deterministic, accurate logic execution outside the language model's text-only reasoning. If you need to run a specific database query, validate a credit score against a threshold, or perform a calculation, a tool can do this reliably every time. Tools reduce the context load on the model by retrieving information on demand rather than requiring everything upfront. Most importantly, tools enable scalable, production-ready workflows where the model becomes truly action-capable instead of just being bound by context limitations. With tools, you can build agents that are more reliable, more consistent, and also capable of handling complex real-world scenarios across both known and new customer situations. Now, this concept of tool-using agents represents the next evolution in building intelligent AI systems, and it also opens up a world of possibilities for creating sophisticated applications that can interact with external systems and data sources in meaningful ways.
