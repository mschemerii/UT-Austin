Let's now discuss the limitations of relying only on reasoning to enhance our AI agent, and explore how planning can overcome these challenges. Now, throughout our test cases with the reasoning-enabled agent, we observed significant improvements over the tools and memory-only approach. However, reasoning alone still has important limitations that can affect the quality and also consistency of our agent's performance. The first limitation is that reasoning alone can cause the agent to jump between ideas, miss required steps, or forget to gather crucial details. Now, while reasoning allows the agent to think through problems, without structure, that thinking can be scattered or incomplete. The agent might consider one aspect of a problem and then jump to another, and this actually ends up leaving a lot of gaps in its analysis. Now, the second limitation is that reasoning can analyze information well, but it does not inherently organize steps in a logical sequence. Now, this leads to inconsistent or incomplete workflows. One customer interaction might proceed smoothly, while another with similar characteristics might be handled quite differently. Simply because the agent happened to reason through these steps in a different order. The third limitation is that without a defined plan, the agent may overask, underask, or missequence the questions during the complex task. We saw hints of this in our test case, where the agents sometimes ask questions that were not ideally ordered for the conversation flow. The agent knows that it needs to gather information, but it does not have a systematic approach to determining what to ask first and what to ask second, and so on. The fourth limitation is that even strong reasoning is fundamentally reactive. The agent decides what to do based on only the current turn, not on a long-term goal. It responds to what happened rather than working towards a defined objective. Now, this reactive nature means that the agent cannot anticipate what will be needed several turns from now. The fifth limitation becomes apparent in multi-stage tasks. Consider the complete credit card recommendation workflow. Now, the agent must check eligibility based on credit score, verify the default history, compare the customer's spending patterns with available products, and also formulate a personalized recommendation. Now, these stages often collapse when reasoning lacks structure and also foresight. The agent may execute some stages well, but miss others entirely. The final limitation is perhaps most subtle, which is the fact that the agent may still produce correct micro decisions at each individual step, but fail the macro task overall because it never mapped out the full path from beginning to end. Each small decision seems reasonable in isolation, but they don't really add up to a coherent whole. Now let's take a look to see how planning overcomes these limitations. So we start off by understanding that planning converts complex tasks into clear multi-step strategies that guide the agent from start to finish. Instead of improvising at each turn, the agent knows its destination and also has a roadmap to get there. Planning ensures that the agent knows what to do first, what to do next, and also what to do last. This sequencing dramatically improves the reliability because the same type of request will be handled consistently following the same structured approach. Planning also coordinates the reasoning, memory, and tools under a unified roadmap. This reduces errors and also repetition because each capability is deployed at the right moment in the overall workflow instead of being used ad hoc. Planning also helps the agent proactively anticipate missing information and gather it in the right order instead of realizing mid conversation that it needs information and it should have asked for this information earlier. The agent now plans ahead to collect everything that it needs in a logical sequence. Planning also maintains consistency across long conversations, ensuring that the final output aligns with the task's ultimate goal. Even in extended multi-turn interactions, the agent stays on track because it is working from a plan instead of simply reacting to each message. Finally, planning transforms the agent from a smart responder into a purpose-driven problem solver. Now, this transformation is essential for high-stakes, multi-turn decision making, like credit card recommendations, where customers expect thoughtful, comprehensive guidance instead of a series of disconnected responses.
